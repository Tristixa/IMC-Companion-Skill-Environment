## Visual language

Aim for a believable capture of a modest real-time stylized JRPG environment. Preserve smooth curves and appealing silhouettes with simple construction; low detail does not mean cubes, voxels, toy blocks or crude primitives.

| Element | Target treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Roofs | Broad curved surfaces, thick readable eaves, a few broad seam bands; omit individual tiny shingles. |
| Walls and structure | Plain plaster/color fields, a few substantial timber supports, simple recessed openings; no dense grain or ornamental lattice. |
| Stone and paving | Quiet large slabs and limited joints; no field of tiny cobbles, chips or noisy brick detail. |
| Foliage | Rounded irregular canopy groups, a few broad shade families, restrained edge detail; no individually rendered leaf field. |
| Props | A few identifiable items at points of use; avoid dozens of tiny goods, flowers, banners and fittings. |
| Cloth and metal | A few broad folds or plane changes; simple fittings with restrained highlights. |
| Lighting | Broad form shading and readable cast shadows; neutral materials, minimal gloss, no painterly finishing, atmospheric blur or cinematic effects. |
| Age and use | A small number of legible repairs, adapted extensions, worn approaches and purposeful storage; avoid random grime or pristine repeated boxes. |

Fantasy identity should survive in the main shapes and construction. Apply this detail level to interiors, wilderness and expedition settings using their own materials and functions. Different places retain their own colors, culture, terrain and atmosphere.

## Camera and output type

Rendering and camera are separate. The approved Eurydica view supplies the accepted wide, elevated isometric-like framing for that section. For a new place, carry forward the requested or established gameplay camera and fixed/scrolling scope. Do not impose the city's exact angle on every interior or battle backdrop.

An early assembled concept can include the whole scene and contextual water when appropriate to the requested preview. Production bases still follow the rendering contract: separate placeable objects, runtime-controlled water and emissions, correct source density, usable ground and verified camera alignment. A pretty image does not establish that these systems are implemented.

## Reference priority and past failure

The Eurydica v0.1 illustration and v0.2 isometric image were too detailed. Changing the camera, adding the word "gameplay", or naming the old 3D-to-2D style did not solve it. Do not use those images as rendering standards.

Default reference order: approved v0.3 appearance, actual game environment complexity, then separately labeled location/design and camera references. When an architectural source is ornate, state that only its silhouettes, construction ideas or geography transfer. Omit old portrait/hall illustration references from default calls: they previously pulled the result toward illustration. Retain them only for explicitly requested alternative styles.

## Review before delivery

Compare the result visually with both retained references at comparable display size. Check plain surfaces, foliage grouping, shadow breadth, sparse props and readable ground. If it still resembles the rejected richly illustrated city, revise it rather than describing it as game-like. Preserve curved fantasy silhouettes during simplification. Report implementation limits only where relevant; do not claim a technical performance budget from an image.
