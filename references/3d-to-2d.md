## Transfer into playable assets

- Build appealing solid volumes and resolve them into drawing. Combine decisive shadow boundaries with gentle changes across broad surfaces; neither blanket flat shading nor airbrushed detail everywhere matches the examples.
- Use edges selectively. A corner, join or silhouette may need emphasis; every floorboard and prop does not need the same dark outline.
- Separate material families through color, plane changes and a few deliberate highlights. Metal, plaster, stone, wood and cloth should remain distinguishable without exhaustive texture.
- Organize warm and cool colors over large areas. Do not turn this into mandatory cinematic blue-orange lighting; hue relationships in authored surfaces can carry much of the appeal.
- Preserve regional fantasy shape language and maintained wear together. A repaired counter or reused beam can have polished drawing and convincing volume.
- Adapt depth to the actual gameplay camera and room size. The portraits' low cameras, monumental distances, glossy floor reflections and background softness are not requirements for a playable room.
- Leave emissions, flames, light pools, shafts and water to the runtime/separate layers as specified in the main contract. Do not remove neutral form shading and material color while removing those effects.

## Review against the failed tavern interpretation

The first Eurydica Tavern test failed as a rendering reference: uniform brown room surfaces, repeated outlined boards and detached-looking modular props did not reproduce the examples' volume, color relationships and material contrast. It also received no image reference in its generation call. Do not reuse it as a target or equate its tabletop-like appearance with the correct downward gameplay camera.

Review the whole assembled scene for consistent object scale, contact and coherent shading, not just each isolated prop. Keep modular assets for production, but judge them as parts of a place. The 3D-to-2D correction is not yet visually validated by a new generation; do not describe this written brief as a proven rendering result.
