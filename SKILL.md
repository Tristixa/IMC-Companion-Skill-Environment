# IMC Environment Art Direction

This companion supplies IMC's environment creative and integration requirements. The installed `sprite-gen` skill supplies image production workflows where needed; keep upstream tooling and provider defaults separate from this art brief. Use `$imc-art-direction` for character and creature deliverables in a mixed request.

Do not generate images during documentation-only work. A candidate request does not itself authorize replacing live textures or changing runtime geometry.

Choose the relevant environment use:

- **Expedition backdrops:** Use this term for non-traversable scenery behind expedition observation or battle encounters. Establish the region/biome, encounter mood, intended camera, party/enemy staging, shared ground plane and HUD space from the actual scene. Design clear combat space and readable silhouettes at gameplay size. Do not assume the backdrop is traversable or that a battle reference's camera applies to every expedition scene.
- **Playable interiors:** Use this term for enclosed traversable places such as the Tavern, Adventurer Office and other HQ rooms.
- **Playable exteriors:** Use this term for traversable outdoor places such as the Company HQ Courtyard, city streets and exploration grounds.
- **Playable environments:** Use this as the parent term for playable interiors and playable exteriors. Reserve roughly 80% open, usable space and about 20% landscape/scenery occupancy. Use a sparse base environment plus separate object sheets by default. Preserve the location's activity, department ownership, entrances, connections and interaction points without crowding circulation. Record geometry changes needed by a redesigned layout.
- **HQ and region maps:** Read the current map documents and UI placement. Preserve meaningful connections, navigation landmarks and clickable areas when required by the task. Keep labels and controls in the runtime UI; a map request does not imply new locations or gameplay rules.

Before generating an environment, resolve its camera and whether the playable camera moves from the current request or established run; ask only for missing choices. For spaces hosting the new IMC world sprites, use the agreed elevated orthographic top-down/front-facing projection: view actors and ground from above with parallel projection, not eye-level frontage or an automatically substituted isometric/perspective view. Exact elevation and location framing still require a coherent scene plan; do not infer them from rejected tavern candidates. Carry the same camera into the base and all object sheets. Other deliberately requested backdrop cameras remain scoped to those scenes.

Classify a playable environment with **no camera movement** as a fixed single-screen area and use a **1920×1080 final production master** unless the verified runtime requires another size. Classify one with **camera movement** as a scrolling large area such as a whole city, district or broad exploration ground. For the current IMC city-scale projection, use **3072×2048** as the minimum final production master; inspect the actual world extent and closest camera zoom and increase the master proportionally when the area is larger. Provider output size is not automatically the final master size. Object sheets may use smaller transparent canvases, but they must retain the base's camera and source pixel density so assembly does not require arbitrary resizing.

Proceed with reasonable recorded choices when context resolves the rest of the brief. Ask only when a missing reference, conflicting design source or unspecified purpose would materially change the result.

## Composition and integration

For a new or redesigned environment, develop an original composition appropriate to its function. Existing room art supplies context and is not a mandatory image-generation reference. Lock existing image coordinates only for a layout-preserving replacement. Read runtime geometry to distinguish functional constraints from an old crop or furnishing arrangement.

Make inhabited places feel owned, used and accumulated rather than newly unpacked. Establish three visible layers: the location's past through restrained repairs, replacement materials or reuse; its present owner through practical adaptations and recognizable organization; and its daily routine through circulation wear, work zones, loading paths and storage placed where it is used. Use controlled imperfection, not broken construction, random grime, procedural distress or dense clutter. For remote natural environments, express age, weather and ecology; add human traces only when the brief establishes travel or occupation.

Keep IMC's other-world fantasy identity explicit. Steambot-inspired nostalgia concerns ownership, maintenance and everyday routines; it does not impose a medieval era, industrial era, rustic inn template or brown palette. Establish a few coherent local design traits through architecture, materials, colors and practical adaptations. Follow [fantasy identity](references/rendering-contract.md#fantasy-identity-and-ownership) without inventing new world lore or filling the playable area with magical decoration.

For playable environments, follow [open space and modular environment assets](references/rendering-contract.md#open-space-and-modular-environment-assets): keep approximately 80% negative space as broad, unobstructed playable ground, with restrained surface detail and about 20% landscape/scenery occupancy. Keep city roads and circulation routes wide. Produce buildings, walls, trees, lamps, stages, flags, banners, crates and other props on separate transparent object sheets; do not bake them into the base. Review the assembled scene too, so adding separate objects does not consume the reserved open space. The ratio is a composition target, not an exact pixel count or a universal rule for non-playable maps and illustrations.

Lock the selected camera before producing a base or modular object sheet. The base and every separate object must use the same projection, camera elevation, horizontal camera angle, horizon/vanishing behavior and ground-plane angle. Objects may rotate within that shared scene. Use the selected or approved base as the camera reference for later object sheets whenever possible. Reject objects drawn from a different camera; correct only the mismatched object when the base is already accepted.

Match intended aspect ratio, locked camera perspective and actual display scale. Keep a usable actor ground plane, natural staging and space for runtime UI. Keep characters and their contact shadows separate from empty backgrounds. Shadows belonging to separate objects must remain separately editable with those objects; terrain shading may stay in the base. Review currently approved sprites at gameplay size with separate contact shadows; previous Tristitia base-sprite approvals were revoked on 2026-09-18 and those candidates are not visual or camera anchors. If no matching approved sprite exists, state the provisional review limitation rather than claiming integration passed. Do not bake extra people, monsters, labels, controls, unrequested text, pseudo-writing, signatures or watermarks into the environment. An explicitly requested inscription is its own intentional element.

Render lamp posts, lantern fixtures, braziers and similar light-bearing objects in an **unlit state**. Do not paint emissive flames, glowing glass, light pools, halos, bloom, lens flare, godrays, volumetric shafts or comparable lighting effects into the base or object sheet; Godot owns those effects. Retain neutral form shading needed to describe the object, but do not imply that the fixture is emitting light.

Keep rivers, ponds, canals, pools, waterfalls and other water surfaces out of the base. The base may describe banks, basins, channels, riverbeds, waterfall cliffs and receiving terrain, but it must contain no painted water surface, foam, spray, reflection or caustics. Prefer runtime water in Godot. When illustrated water is specifically required, deliver it as a separate transparent object/layer with its placement, mask, scale and correction path recorded.

Follow the rendering contract for foreground occlusion, foot contact and separate runtime shadows. Retain the base and independently editable object assets as production deliverables even when the current renderer needs a flattened composite. Asset separation does not imply implemented parallax, collision, movement or occlusion systems. For a redesign, record required walkable-area, entrance, interaction, actor-position and occlusion adjustments before an authorized integration.

Keep runs and candidate revisions separate from live game assets. Save the location brief and reference provenance with the run, retain source references during authorized production, and mark candidate versus approved output explicitly. Diagnose rendering/import issues before regenerating approved artwork. A technically valid file or prompt style label does not establish visual quality.

Deliver the requested candidate/export with relevant paths, checks actually performed and any remaining integration work. For documentation/skill edits, list authored files and their purpose.
